# Free and open-source instrument banks for Synthia

Research date: 2026-11-11. Status: catalogue only. Nothing was downloaded, installed or wired into the app.

Synthia runs Tone.js 15 in the browser and will play only sampled instruments and drums from banks we are allowed to download (scope set 2026-11-11: the synthesizer is out for now, so synth material is reduced to Appendix A). It is for Mauro's personal, non-commercial use, but a publicly served build redistributes every sample it loads, so the **Re-host publicly?** column matters as much as the sound.

## How to read this

- **Sources.** Every licence and quality claim comes from a page, README, LICENSE file or GitHub/npm API response fetched on the research date. Claims that could not be confirmed are marked UNVERIFIED. Sizes come from HEAD requests, the GitHub API (`size` includes git history) or the source's own figure. The lead re-fetched the decisive licence quotes for the top picks. "sfzi-data" means the third-party catalogue at `sfzinstruments.github.io` (`data/sfz/instruments.yml`), which is weaker evidence than the author's own page.
- **Grades.**
  - **A**: high quality and usable now, with a clean licence.
  - **B**: good, with one real limitation (licence, size, few layers, or conversion work).
  - **C**: usable but mediocre, or with no stated licence.
  - Anything worse is listed under "Excluded".
- **Web-readiness.**
  - "loads as-is in Tone.Sampler": per-note files at stable URLs.
  - "needs <library>": a named player library is required.
  - "needs conversion (<kind>)": the files must be transformed first.
- **Tone.Sampler limits** (from `Tone/instrument/Sampler.ts`):
  - It loads one buffer per note and pitch-shifts the nearest one with `playbackRate`.
  - `velocity` only scales gain.
  - There are no velocity layers and no round robins.
  - Loop points come only from the `loop`/`loopStart`/`loopEnd` options.
  - As a result, every multi-velocity bank below says "needs …".
- **Deduplication.** A bank appears once, under its most natural category, with its mirrors named. Two cases appear twice on purpose:
  - §4 (Web-Ready) lists the *web packagings* of banks from §1–§3 (a different artefact on a different host, often with a mislabelled licence).
  - VCSL percussion appears in §2 (Drums) for browsing.

Entry count: 114 graded entries: A 20, B 54, C 40; per section: §1 7/10/1; §2 5/8/2; §3 3/5/2; §4 2/11/4; §6 3/2/5; §7 0/5/6; §8 0/13/20. Appendix A (synth, out of scope) adds 1/7/5 (A/B/C).

## Packs: bundled vs local-only

Rule (agreed with synthia-1d on 2026-11-11, now that the repository is public at github.com/msdavid/synthia):

- Only banks whose **Re-host publicly?** cell is "yes" may be committed to git or ship with a publicly served build. Those are marked **bundled**.
- Banks with no licence, an unclear licence, or a licence that forbids re-hosting go in a separate **local-only** pack. That pack is never committed to git and never included in a publicly served build. The loader must treat it as optional.
- The **Pack** column is derived mechanically from the Re-host cell. A row that groups several banks takes its weakest member's pack: in the Terkelsen/MSLP/Ixox/Winer row only the marimba lacks a licence. Players (§6) carry no pack, because they are code dependencies. These bundled entries carry a condition to honour:
  - Credit lines for every CC BY / BY-SA bank.
  - Share-alike for converted BY-SA files (AVL, Salamander Drumkit).
  - jRhodes3 is CC BY-NC: bundled only while Synthia stays non-commercial.
  - Splendid Grand rests on an untraced public-domain claim.
  - University of Iowa has a grant but no licence document.

## Download and personal use

Since the scope change, the first question for every bank is whether its licence clearly lets Mauro download it and use it privately. Public hosting is the second question (the **Re-host** column).

- **Clearly yes:**
  - Everything under CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, MIT or the Unlicense, plus GeneralUser GS.
  - University of Iowa: "downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions".
  - Philharmonia: "free to use these samples as you wish".
  - Pianobook: use is free, but redistribution is forbidden.
  - jRhodes3 (CC BY-NC).
  - Arachno, Timbres of Heaven and Musyng Kite: private or personal use.
  - Legowelt: "free to download and use in your productions".
  - 99Sounds: royalty-free.
  - Goldbaby, Samples From Mars and Wave Alchemy: use in music. Samples From Mars needs an email address and Wave Alchemy needs an account.
  - Decent Samples, MusicRadar SampleRadar and BPB packs: use in music, no redistribution.
  - §8 also has a per-row verdict. Only Aegean Symphonic Orchestra is unclear there.
- **Yes for private use, but they could never be hosted publicly:** Philharmonia, Pianobook, Goldbaby, Samples From Mars, 99Sounds, Arachno, Timbres of Heaven, Musyng Kite, Decent Samples, SampleRadar, BPB packs.
- **Not clearly permitted** (no licence, or the grant can't be traced). Mauro has approved using these anyway (see Decisions):
  - No licence or provenance: tidal-drum-machines, Dirt-Samples, Drum Abuse, danigb's other drum machines.
  - Mellotron Archive CD-ROM: `"license": "unknown"`, and it looks like a commercial CD.
  - Untraceable grants: smpldsnds/drum-machines and oramics (self-applied "public domain"), Terkelsen Marimba (no licence).
  - Weak or indirect statements: Technopolis TR-808 ("ABSOLUTELY FREE!" with no grant), Taijiguy Mellotron ("free as in beer"), DX7 factory ROMs (Yamaha data), SGM and FatBoy (no primary licence fetched), Magenta `sgm_plus` (licence chain unverified).

## Decisions (settled 2026-11-11)

1. **Player:** per-note files plus a JSON map, played by our own thin velocity-layer / round-robin sampler on Tone.js. No smplr or spessasynth_lib for now, so the standardized-audio-context spike is not needed. docs/research/libraries.md reached the same conclusion independently.
2. **Hosting:**
   - No hotlinking.
   - No audio files in git, whether bundled or local-only.
   - Every bank is fetched into a local, git-ignored `samples/` folder by a script driven by a manifest committed to the repo. Each manifest entry carries the source URL, file filter, licence, credit line and pack.
   - "Bundled" and "local-only" use the same mechanism. The pack only decides what a public deploy may serve.
3. **Public deploy:**
   - Synthia will be used by other people from a public address, non-commercially.
   - A public build serves **bundled** banks only.
   - Non-commercial licences (CC BY-NC, BY-NC-SA) are acceptable.
   - Banks with no licence, or that forbid re-hosting, never ship. Mauro approved using them locally, and users can add them themselves through import.
4. **Size:** start small and curated, and lazy-load per instrument.
5. **Credits:** every bank's credit line lives in the manifest, so the in-app credits page can be generated from it.
6. **Imported packs are stored as the original audio files, untouched.** No transcoding on import: fastest import, no quality loss, no loop drift. A "compact" option can come later if storage becomes a problem.
7. **Freesound:** the fetch script takes an optional token from an environment variable. Without it, the Freesound-hosted instruments are skipped with a clear message and the rest of the starter set still installs. Those rows are marked "needs Freesound token".
8. **Starter-set headroom goes to quality:** Salamander at 8 layers and pizzicato for the string sections, staying under 300 MB.
9. **Import phases** stand as proposed. Kontakt, gig and sfArk are documented as "convert first with ConvertWithMoss".

## Importing a pack

Requirement (Mauro, via synthia-1d): users import packs they found online into their own browser storage (OPFS). The aim is to support every format in circulation, added one at a time behind a single import pipeline. The most popular formats are mandatory. Researched 2026-11-11. ### Notes (from the research pass)

1. **SF2/SF3.** `SoundBankLoader.fromArrayBuffer(buf)` then `bank.samples[i].getAudioData()` (mono Float32), `.sampleRate`, `.loopStart/.loopEnd` (sample points), `releaseData()` to free memory; `audioToWav(...)` exists (example `extract_first_sample_as_wav.ts`). Zones carry `keyRange`, `velRange`, `generators[]`, `modulators[]`; instrument zones link a sample. We write the flattening (preset zone + instrument zone, generator sums to tune, gain, ADSR, loop mode). SF3 decode is internal (stb-vorbis, wasm), so no browser Ogg decoder is needed. `getRawData(true)` returns the raw Vorbis stream, but native `<audio>` Vorbis only reached Safari 18.4 (caniuse), so always decode with the library. Alternatives checked: `soundfont2` (MIT, last push 2024-10-08, SF3 not mentioned in README) and `@marmooo/soundfont-parser` (MIT, 2026-09-04, SF2+SF3 parser, 3 stars). Prefer spessasynth_core.
2. **SFZ opcodes that matter** (sfzformat.com source, CC0, pushed 2026-06-08): `lokey/hikey/key`, `pitch_keycenter` (value `sample` means read the WAV metadata, defaults to note 0 if absent), `lovel/hivel`, `seq_length`/`seq_position` (counter per region, max 100; silent steps if mappings are irregular), `loop_mode` (`no_loop`, `one_shot`, `loop_continuous`, `loop_sustain`; unset = use loop metadata of the audio file), `loop_start/loop_end`, `trigger=release|release_key` (forced one_shot; behaviour varies per player), `#define $VAR`, `#include "file"` (paths relative to the main SFZ, nestable), `sw_last`/`sw_default` (ARIA). Parser must: expand includes and defines first, then walk `<control> <global> <master> <group> <region>` with inheritance (sfz-tools-core does this merge too), resolve `sample=` paths (case and slash tolerance is our job), and read the smpl chunk for loops and `pitch_keycenter=sample`. Hard parts: path matching on case-insensitive packs, `default_path`, `lorand/hirand` random RR, `ampeg_*` mapping, CC-conditional regions. sfizz (BSD-2-Clause, push 2025-03-17) is the behavioural reference for ARIA.
3. **Decent Sampler** docs: https://decentsampler-developers-guide.readthedocs.io (v1.42.1; the old decentsamples.com page just redirects). The `<sample>` element documents `path`, `rootNote`, `loNote`, `hiNote`, `loVel`, `hiVel`, `seqMode`, `seqPosition`, `seqLength` (round robins need both seqMode and seqPosition on every sample), `loopStart`, `loopEnabled`, `loopCrossfade`, `trigger="release"`; the docs also cover keyswitch topics. Group/sample inheritance applies like SFZ. So it is nearly 1:1 with our model: trivial except dropping the UI and effects.
4. **Loose audio, zip, folder.**
   - fflate README table: streaming and "up to 4 GB" are ticked for fflate, plus async worker use and streaming ZIP; 8 kB core. zip.js README: streams, Zip64 for archives over 4 GB, split zips, encryption, `BlobReader`/`BlobWriter` (can read from a `File` without loading it all). JSZip: streaming/limits not confirmed (UNVERIFIED), likely whole-archive in RAM. Recommendation: zip.js for user-picked zips (random access over a `File`), fflate for small internal archives (xrni, adg, multisample, tar.gz).
   - `showDirectoryPicker`: Chrome 86+, Firefox no, Safari no (MDN BCD). `<input webkitdirectory>`: Chrome 7, Firefox 50, Safari 11.1; iOS Safari only from 18.4 (earlier it is set but has no effect). So `webkitdirectory` is the baseline, `showDirectoryPicker` an optional Chromium nicety.
   - Filename conventions: no standard exists that I found. ConvertWithMoss documents what it does: read note and range from WAV/AIFF/CAF instrument chunks first; else parse a note name or MIDI number from the filename; "hard"-style words; equal split of detected groups across velocity (2 groups = 0-63 and 64-127). We write ~150 LOC: note name regex (C#3 / Db4 / MIDI number), `v\d+`/`vel\d+`/`_ff_` dynamics, `rr\d+`/`_a_` RR. No JS library found. The UI must let the user fix guesses (a mapping preview table).
5. **Decode and storage limits (all formats).**
   - `decodeAudioData` resamples to the context rate (MDN). caniuse (about the `<audio>` element, NOT decodeAudioData): WAV all browsers; FLAC Chrome 56, Firefox 51, Safari 13; Ogg Vorbis Chrome 4, Firefox 3.5, Safari 18.4 only; Opus Chrome 33, Firefox 15, Safari partial (macOS 15.4+, iOS 18.4+). MDN codec guide: AAC/ALAC patchy (ALAC Safari only). WAV 24-bit and AIFF in `decodeAudioData`: UNVERIFIED, so decode PCM ourselves (RIFF/AIFF parse, ~100 LOC) and keep FLAC/Vorbis on the native or wasm path.
   - Quotas (MDN): Chrome/Edge up to 60% of disk per origin; Firefox min(10% of disk, 10 GiB per site group) best-effort, 50% persistent; Safari ~60% of disk (browser apps), ~15% in embedded WebViews; older Safari 1 GiB then prompt. Safari evicts script-written data after 7 days without interaction (when tracking prevention is on); `navigator.storage.persist()` exempts the origin. persist(): Chrome 55, Firefox 57 (prompt), Safari 15.2; not available in Workers; Chrome and Safari decide silently.
   - `FileSystemSyncAccessHandle`: dedicated Workers only, OPFS only; Chrome 102, Firefox 111, Safari 15.2. A read-write handle is exclusive per file (a second open throws `NoModificationAllowedError`); read-only handles may be shared across tabs.
   - Transcode to Opus: `AudioEncoder` (WebCodecs) Chrome 94, Firefox 130, Safari 26 (BCD); whether each supports the `opus` codec: UNVERIFIED, test with `AudioEncoder.isConfigSupported()` at runtime. Output is raw packets, so we need an Ogg/WebM muxer and, for playback, `AudioDecoder` or a container `decodeAudioData` accepts. Lossy re-encode risks loop-point drift and double loss on already-lossy packs; I would store FLAC-class or raw PCM first and treat Opus as an opt-in size saver (judgement, not sourced).
6. **Existing converter as oracle.** ConvertWithMoss (git-moss, Java, LGPL-3.0, pushed 2026-11-08) reads and writes about 60 formats and documents each. Use its docs/design notes as spec for Akai, DWP, Kontakt; do not copy code into a non-LGPL app. It is also the fallback for users with exotic packs: convert to SFZ or SF2 offline, then import.
7. **Our manifest** (export and import must round-trip) must carry: manifest version; instrument id, name, author, licence, source format and file; per sample: id, OPFS path, sha256, container/codec, sample rate, channels, bit depth, length, original filename, loop (mode, start, end, crossfade) plus the root note and tuning found in file metadata; per region: sample id, key lo/hi/root, vel lo/hi, RR group + position + length (and mode), tune cents, gain dB, pan, offset/end, trigger (attack/release), mute/choke group, optional ADSR, optional articulation id (key-switch split), plus an `extras` bag for lost opcodes so a re-export can warn. Samples are referenced by id so Tone.js can map them lazily.

### One import pipeline

1. **Source adapter**: turns whatever the user gave (File, zip via zip.js, `webkitdirectory` FileList, directory handle) into one virtual file tree: `list()`, `read(path) -> File/Blob` (lazy, no full-pack decode), case-insensitive lookup.
2. **Detect**: by extension, then magic bytes (`RIFF....sfbk`, `RIFF....DLS `, `OggS`, `fLaC`, gzip `1f8b`, zip `PK`), then by sentinel file (`*.sfz`, `*.dspreset`, `multisample.xml`, `drumkit.xml`, `Instrument.xml`). Returns candidate converters plus a confidence; the user confirms if ambiguous.
3. **Converter** (one module per format, pure, no storage): `convert(tree) -> AsyncIterable<{sample: {id, bytes|pcm, rate, channels, loop?}} | {region} | {meta} | {lost: string}>`. It emits our manifest records and the list of dropped features (shown in the import report). The loose-audio converter is the fallback for any tree.
4. **Normalise (Web Worker)**: store the original files untouched (decision 6). Only extracted-from-container samples (SF2/SF3/DLS PCM) are written out, as WAV or FLAC. Decoding (via `decodeAudioData`, our own PCM/AIFF code, or the library decoder) happens at play time, one sample at a time to cap memory.
5. **Store (dedicated Worker)**: write each sample with `createSyncAccessHandle` under `/packs/<id>/samples/`, then write `manifest.json` last (a pack without a manifest is an interrupted import and is garbage collected), call `navigator.storage.persist()` from the main thread, show `navigator.storage.estimate()`.
6. **Runtime**: manifest -> Synthia's sampler map; samples are read from OPFS on demand.

### Phases

1. **Phase 1:**
   - The pipeline skeleton, our manifest and the OPFS store.
   - Loose audio, zip and folder import, with WAV chunk reading and filename guessing (S).
   - SF2/SF3/DLS via spessasynth_core (M).
   - SFZ core opcodes (M).

   Together these unlock nearly everything in the catalogue.
2. **Phase 2:** Decent Sampler, Hydrogen, Bitwig `.multisample` and Renoise `.xrni` (S each: XML in a zip or tar, reusing the phase 1 plumbing), plus SFZ extras (crossfades, key-switch split into articulations, release layers).
3. **Phase 3:** Ableton `.adg`/`.adv` (unencrypted Sampler/Simpler presets only) and `.sf2pack`. A "compact" storage option (transcoding) comes only if storage becomes a problem, since imports keep the original files.
4. **Phase 4** (binary parsers from ConvertWithMoss's design notes, only if users ask): Akai `.akp`, DirectWave `.dwp`, EXS24 `.exs`.
5. **Not feasible in the browser:**
   - Kontakt `.nki`/`.ncw`: proprietary and reverse-engineered, and encrypted libraries can't be read at all.
   - `.gig`: GPL-only C++ library.
   - sfArk: GPL C++, abandoned 2018.
   - Ableton's encrypted packs.

   Document these as **"convert first with ConvertWithMoss"** (LGPL-3.0, Java, ~60 formats, pushed 2026-11-08): convert offline to SFZ or SF2, then import. Use its documents as specs, but don't copy its code into Synthia.

### Must-test in the first import spike

Imported files are stored untouched (decision 6), so these decode questions decide which originals play directly:
1. `decodeAudioData` on 24-bit WAV and on AIFF in Chrome (the target), and also in Firefox. If either fails, use our own RIFF/AIFF PCM reader (~100 lines).
2. FLAC and Ogg Vorbis/Opus decoding through `decodeAudioData` in each browser. caniuse only covers the `<audio>` element. Safari has no Web MIDI and patchy Ogg support, so it is not the target.

`AudioEncoder` Opus support matters only for a later "compact" option.
